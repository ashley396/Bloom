SEASONAL FLORIST PLANNING

Valentine's Day prep for flower shops

Valentine's Day is the single highest-volume day most flower shops see all year, compressed into one or two days of fulfillment. Planning starts weeks earlier, not the week of.

Seasonal PlanningValentine's Day

By the Florisyn Team · Published August 20, 2026

Timeline: what to lock down and when

  1. 6–8 weeks out

    Place wholesale pre-orders for red and premium stems — the highest-demand varieties sell out or spike in price closest to the date, so early ordering protects both supply and margin.

  2. 3–4 weeks out

    Finalize your Valentine's product lineup and pricing, confirm staffing (extra designers, drivers, and phone coverage), and open online/phone ordering with a clear delivery-date cutoff.

  3. 1–2 weeks out

    Confirm delivery zones and routing capacity for the day itself — Valentine's Day delivery volume routinely outstrips a shop's normal daily capacity, so plan routes and driver shifts in advance rather than day-of.

  4. 2–3 days out

    Receive and process incoming stems, confirm cooler space can hold the volume, and finalize the day's build schedule so designers aren't building same-day under pressure.

  5. The day itself

    Run a single source of truth for order status — same-day changes and last-minute walk-ins are common, and a shop-wide view of what's built, what's out for delivery, and what's still pending prevents duplicate or missed orders.

What tends to go wrong

  • Underordering premium red roses, then paying a last-minute premium or substituting without telling the customer
  • Taking more delivery orders than the driver schedule can actually cover for the date
  • No cutoff time for same-day orders, so the queue never actually closes
  • Cooler space maxed out before the day even starts, forcing rushed early builds that lose freshness by delivery time

None of this is Florisyn-specific — it's true whatever system a shop runs on. Where it connects: a recipe-based product catalog and delivery routing built for a single high-volume day help, but the planning itself has to happen regardless of the software behind it.

Common questions

How far out should I order roses for Valentine's Day?

Many shops place initial wholesale commitments 6–8 weeks out, since premium red roses are the variety most likely to sell out or spike in price close to the date.

Should I set a cutoff for online orders?

A clear delivery-date cutoff (rather than leaving ordering open indefinitely) is a common way shops keep the day's volume inside what staff and drivers can actually fulfill.

What if I run out of a variety I promised?

Deciding your substitution policy in advance — and being upfront with the customer if a substitution happens — avoids a same-day scramble and an unhappy customer.
