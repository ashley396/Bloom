SEASONAL FLORIST PLANNING

Holiday season planning for flower shops

The winter holidays run as one long season for most flower shops — Thanksgiving centerpieces, Christmas gift orders and greenery, and a New Year's tail — rather than a single peak day.

Seasonal PlanningHolidays

By the Florisyn Team · Published August 20, 2026

The shape of the season

  • Thanksgiving week brings centerpiece demand concentrated into a few days before the holiday
  • December spreads out more than Valentine's or Mother's Day — gift orders, corporate accounts, and holiday parties arrive across the whole month rather than one weekend
  • Seasonal greenery, wreaths, and holiday-specific product lines add inventory categories that don't exist the rest of the year
  • Year-end is also when many shops reconcile the books for tax season, on top of the operational volume

Planning through the season

  1. Early fall

    Plan your holiday product lineup (wreaths, centerpieces, gift arrangements) and place wholesale pre-orders for seasonal greenery and holiday varieties.

  2. Mid-November

    Open Thanksgiving centerpiece ordering with a clear pickup/delivery cutoff, given the short window before the holiday itself.

  3. Early December

    Reach out to corporate accounts and repeat customers for holiday orders before their internal budgets or calendars fill up.

  4. Through December

    Track order volume against production capacity week by week rather than waiting until the week of Christmas to notice you're overbooked.

  5. Year-end

    Reconcile inventory and sales for the season while records are fresh, rather than reconstructing them later for tax purposes.

Common questions

Is December really busier than a single holiday like Valentine's Day?

It's a different shape of busy — spread across weeks rather than concentrated in a day or two — but the total volume across Thanksgiving through New Year's is significant for most shops.

Should I set up a separate holiday product line?

Many shops do add seasonal items (wreaths, holiday centerpieces) that don't exist the rest of the year, which means new recipes and pricing to set up, not just a seasonal color swap on existing products.

When should I start reaching out to corporate accounts for holiday orders?

Early December is common, before other vendors and internal budgets are locked in for the season.
