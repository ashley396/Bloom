How to start a flower shop: a practical checklist

Opening a flower shop involves a lot of small decisions that compound. This is a practical starting checklist, not a guarantee, your local requirements and market will shape the specifics.

Florist BusinessStarting a Shop

Before you open

  1. Choose a business structure & check local licensing

    Requirements vary by city and state; confirm what your local government and any resale/seller's permit process actually require before you commit to a location.

  2. Find your wholesale suppliers

    Build relationships with two or three reliable wholesalers rather than depending on one. See our guide to buying wholesale flowers.

  3. Decide your initial product mix & recipe costs

    Work out the actual ingredient cost of your core arrangements before you set prices, not after.

  4. Set up a way to take and track orders

    Even a simple system beats a notebook once order volume grows past what one person can remember.

  5. Plan your opening inventory conservatively

    Flowers are perishable; buy light for your first weeks and adjust once you know real demand.

  6. Set your pricing model

    Base prices on cost of goods, labor, and overhead, not just what feels reasonable. See our guide to pricing floral arrangements.

  7. Get the word out locally before opening day

    Local visibility, a real website, and word of mouth typically matter more early on than broad advertising.

None of this is legal or financial advice, requirements differ by location and business structure, so confirm specifics with your local licensing office and a qualified advisor.

Common questions

How much does it cost to start a flower shop?

Costs vary widely by market, location, and whether you lease a storefront or start smaller, so there's no single reliable number. Build a real budget around your specific location and plan before committing.

Do I need formal floral design training?

It isn't strictly required, many florists learn through apprenticeship or on-the-job experience, though formal programs exist and can help build a foundation.

Can I start without a physical storefront?

Yes, some florists start with delivery-only or online-order models before opening a storefront, though local licensing requirements still apply.
