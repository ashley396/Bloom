SEASONAL FLORIST PLANNING

Mother's Day prep for flower shops

Mother's Day is usually the second-highest volume day of the year for most flower shops, right behind Valentine's Day — and it comes with its own planning quirks, especially around delivery timing.

Seasonal PlanningMother's Day

What's different about Mother's Day

  • Delivery is concentrated on one Sunday, which is a harder day to staff drivers for than a weekday rush
  • A meaningful share of orders arrive from adult children ordering for mothers in another city — accuracy on delivery address and recipient details matters more than usual
  • Demand skews toward pastel and mixed arrangements rather than a single dominant variety like Valentine's red roses, which spreads wholesale ordering across more stem types
  • Many shops see a secondary bump the Friday and Saturday before, for early deliveries and in-person pickup

Planning timeline

  1. 4–6 weeks out

    Place wholesale pre-orders across your expected mix of varieties — Mother's Day demand is broader than Valentine's, so this is less about one flower selling out and more about having enough overall volume.

  2. 2–3 weeks out

    Confirm driver availability for the Sunday itself, plus the Friday/Saturday lead-in — Sunday delivery staffing is often the tightest constraint of the whole weekend.

  3. 1 week out

    Set and communicate a delivery-date cutoff, and confirm cooler capacity can hold the incoming volume without overcrowding.

  4. The weekend itself

    Track delivery status across Friday, Saturday, and Sunday as one continuous window rather than three separate days, since orders and drivers move across all three.

Common questions

Is Mother's Day really the second-biggest day for florists?

It's widely reported as one of the top two volume days of the year for flower shops, alongside Valentine's Day, though the exact ranking varies shop to shop.

Why is Sunday delivery harder to staff?

Regular delivery staff may not normally work Sundays, so many shops bring on temporary or cross-trained drivers specifically for the weekend.

Should I take orders through Sunday itself?

Some shops close online ordering earlier in the week to protect delivery capacity; others keep it open with a same-day cutoff. Either can work — the key is deciding in advance rather than improvising.
