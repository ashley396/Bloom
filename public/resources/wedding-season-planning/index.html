SEASONAL FLORIST PLANNING

Wedding season planning for florists

Wedding season concentrates a different kind of pressure than a single holiday: instead of one enormous day, it's months of overlapping proposals, deposits, and event-day logistics for multiple clients at once.

Seasonal PlanningWeddings

The core challenge

A single wedding is a manageable project. Wedding season is several of them running in parallel, each with its own proposal, deposit schedule, and delivery/setup timeline — and some weekends carrying more than one event.

What matters most during wedding season

  • A real proposal-to-booking pipeline, so you can see which inquiries are just browsing versus ready to book and pay a deposit
  • Deposit and balance tracking per wedding, not just a single invoice total
  • A production schedule that accounts for the actual build time each wedding needs, not just the delivery date
  • A clear plan for weekends with more than one wedding — staffing, vehicle capacity, and setup/breakdown windows all have to be checked against each other, not planned wedding-by-wedding in isolation
  • A cutoff for how close to the date a new wedding booking can still be accepted

Pricing a wedding fairly to your business

Wedding work often includes significant labor — consultations, mockups, and setup/breakdown time — that's easy to under-price if a proposal is built only around the flowers themselves. Pricing off the same real ingredient-and-labor cost basis used for everyday arrangements, scaled up, tends to hold up better than a flat per-wedding guess. Common questions

How many weddings can one shop handle in a weekend?

It depends entirely on your staffing, vehicle capacity, and design-team size — there's no universal number. The important part is checking capacity across all of a weekend's weddings together, not approving each one in isolation.

How far in advance do most florists book weddings?

Timelines vary widely by market and shop size; many florists take bookings anywhere from several months to over a year out, with a cutoff for how close to the date a new booking can still be accepted.

Should I require a deposit?

Most florists doing wedding work do require some form of deposit to hold a date, given the labor and inventory commitment involved — exact terms vary by shop.
